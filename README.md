# Finite Jest


## Manifesto

Large social media corporations profit from keeping users on the screen for as long as possible. It is profitable to maximize engagement rates, retention rates, and time spent on the app. Due to the nature of social media, users have a near-unlimited supply of content to consume. In the interest of optimizing user time spent on the platform, powerful machine learning models deliver the media that keeps the user on the platform for as long as possible.

Short-form content platforms are especially well-positioned to leverage machine learning. An endless stream of scrollable content creates a low-friction experience with the impression of bottomlessness, catered according to a personal algorithm. This is the user experience that creates the phenomenon known as "doomscrolling".

There are people who make money when users doomscroll. The consequences include a loss of productivity, exacerbation of mental health issues, less time for hobbies. The behaviour is habit-forming. These are hundred-billion-dollar industries with billion-dollar compute invested in keeping users on screens. Screen time limits and app blocks are flimsy ten-dollar measures that rely on the compromised judgement of a user who has been trained, for the last ten minutes, to look at the next most engaging content available.

Weakening the effect of the predatory attention algorithm isn't enough. Telling users to just delete the app, quit, isn't a resilient solution. The decision to scroll is based on immediate reward, and the consequences are delayed.

Finite Jest proposes that a possible solution to fighting doomscrolling habits can, like the campaign against smoking, benefit from educating users about the long-term effects of short-term decisions. Unlike smoking however, those long-term effects are apparent within the day - users scroll and find that hours have been lost. When it comes to what keeps the user scrolling, a billion-dollar model knows more than the user themselves.
